# Competition Notes

## Research Story

- Problem: VUS classifications leave uncertainty, and some change as evidence develops.
- Question: can an earlier snapshot contain explainable clues about a later classification?
- Key rigor: historical evaluation and strict prevention of future-information leakage.
- First evidence of quality: a manually checked cross-release matching process, not an early model score.

## Materials to Prepare Later

- concise abstract with no unsupported clinical claims,
- methods diagram showing releases, matching, outcomes, features, and held-out evaluation,
- class-balance and matching-quality tables,
- baseline comparisons with uncertainty estimates where appropriate,
- confusion matrices and calibration plots,
- examples of correct, incorrect, and ambiguous cases,
- limitations, ethics, and reproducibility statements,
- demonstration website and science-fair poster.

## Records to Preserve

Keep dated notebook entries, source and release details, data dictionaries, failed approaches, matching audits, code versions, environment information, and all decisions that affect inclusion or labels. Check each competition's current rules for prior approval, eligible data use, required forms, display restrictions, and source-code or AI-assistance disclosure.
