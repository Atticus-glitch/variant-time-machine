Question

What V8 tested

V8 used fields available in the January 2024 ClinVar snapshot to predict whether 1,000 records then classified as uncertain later moved toward a benign or pathogenic aggregate classification by July 2026. The sealed test excluded development Variation IDs and predictor-time gene components.

Interpretation

Why the result is promising

V8 retained strong recall for both later directions on a 1,000-record component-disjoint test, rather than achieving high raw accuracy by predicting only the more common benign direction. It also passed the recorded leakage and artifact-integrity audit. This supports continued retrospective study and manual error review, not clinical use.

Limitations that travel with the result

What V8 does not establish

  • The test is outcome-selected and does not predict whether a VUS will resolve.
  • The 1,000 records span 559 predictor-time gene components, not 1,000 independent gene samples.
  • The July 2026 archive had already been accessed for V7, and V8 membership is reconstructible.
  • Component/class weighting, the simplicity tie-break, and grouped out-of-fold reuse limit the development claim.

Use this wording

Strongest truthful claim right now

In a retrospective historical ClinVar experiment, Variant Time Machine predicted later resolved classification direction for older VUS records using only older-snapshot features. V8 achieved 89.5% accuracy and 87.1% balanced accuracy on a 1,000-record gene-component-disjoint retrospective test. However, a paired same-record comparison with V7 showed no statistically clear overall superiority, so the result supports V8 as a strong simplified retrospective model, not as a clinically validated predictor.
